2Motivation

Who has the problem

MBA students recruiting for summer internships. During the school year they apply to many organisations at once, each with its own deadlines and process, and they build relationships with alumni, recruiters and classmates who can open a door. The information they need is not missing: it is scattered. It arrives in at least nine places, and only some of them can be read automatically (Figure 2).

So the hard part is attention and coordination: deciding, every week, which of many applications, people and promised follow-ups deserve the next hour. This project grew out of that problem in the author's own search: he first built a personal tracker for himself, and this app rebuilds it as a separate product any student can use.

How students solve it today

The student is the integration layer. They read each channel, retype what matters into a spreadsheet or a job tracker, and decide from memory what to do next. The trackers that exist are typed by hand: Teal's and Huntr's own pages describe contacts and follow-ups that the user enters (Section 3). School platforms such as 12twenty and Handshake hold postings and events, but not the student's conversations. The decision itself, where to spend this week, is still made in the student's head.

What the current process costs

  • Attention. The useful signal is a small share of the noise. On the author's own accounts, the app's local filter (counting only, nothing sent) passed only 29 of 150 recent Gmail emails, 23 of 125 WhatsApp conversations and 3 of 36 iMessage conversations as touching his target organisations, his contacts or recruiting (EVIDENCE.md, Oct 25). Today a student reads all of it to find that part. This is one student's mailbox; it shows the share, not the minutes.
  • Quality. A follow-up promised in a chat, or a deadline in a recruiter's email, is easy to lose because it never reaches the tracker. We have not yet measured how often this happens; Section 7 sets out how the user test will.
  • Money. Four job-search tools that cover part of the problem charge $29 to $40 a month: Teal+ $29 per 30 days, Simplify+ $39.99, LinkedIn Premium Career $39.99, Huntr Pro $40 (vendor pages, read Oct 26, 2026). None of them reads a student's WhatsApp or iMessage chats.

Why it matters

Recruiting runs on two clocks at once: hard application deadlines and soft relationship timing (a thank-you note within a day, a promised résumé, a contact who offered to flag an application once it is in), both competing with a full course load. A missed follow-up with someone who offered to help, or a deadline noticed two days late, cannot be bought back. A tool that keeps the picture current, shows the evidence for every fact and proposes the few moves worth this week puts scarce attention where it changes outcomes, while every message stays in the student's hands.

What this app does differently

No product we read combines these three things, and each one answers a failure of today's process:

  1. One memory across channels, tied to opportunities. People and dated facts from mail, chats, exports, invites and postings land on the same two boards, each person linked to the internship they can help with. Trackers wait for the student to type; Dex remembers people, not internships; Simplify reads Gmail, and its pages do not mention chats.
  2. A weekly plan with evidence. Plan my week ranks the student's open actions with why now, a first step and the record ids it relied on, and every saved fact carries the exact quote it came from, checked in code. A student can see why the coach says what it says, and correct it.
  3. Read-only by design. The connector opens Gmail and the iMessage and WhatsApp databases read-only, and no agent has a sending tool: drafts wait in an Outbox for the student. Simplify, LinkedIn, Dex (mail merge) and ChatGPT's agents can send or act. In recruiting, one wrong message to a recruiter costs more than any time saved.

ChatGPT is the honest benchmark. A motivated student could connect Gmail to ChatGPT and ask for a weekly summary. They would not get a pipeline that persists and is merged by rules, WhatsApp and iMessage chats filtered on their own computer, facts that must quote their source, or job-board search limited to their targets, at a measured cost below a cent per plan. Where we are weaker: no résumé or autofill tools (Teal, Huntr and Simplify are strong there), far less job data than LinkedIn or Simplify, and automatic syncing of chats needs a Mac.

Model and why

All five agents run on gpt-6-luna through the course's Portkey key. The course key allows no other model, and gpt-6-luna is the low-cost tier ($0.10 per million input tokens, $0.01 cached, $0.50 output; OpenAI's pricing page, read Oct 26). We designed the work to fit it: agents read short, retrieved context and return typed, structured answers that code validates, so correctness does not rest on the model alone. Every agent runs with reasoning effort “low” and an 8,000-token cap per answer. Every response's model name is checked: Portkey answers as gpt-6-luna-global, and an answer from any other model is rejected (agents/runtime.py, tested).

Organisation and delegation

The Career coach is the boss for planning and chat. It never reads the database: it may only rank the candidate actions that code derives from the records, and it learns about them by asking the two specialists through tools, at most 4 questions per request. Each specialist answers from the 10 records that best match the question (BM25 search over its own record type) and may cite only ids it was given; specialists have no delegation tools, so delegation is one level deep and cannot loop. The Inbox organizer, Opportunity scout (discovery) and Setup guide work alone, and code routes “Prepare my call with …” and Who can open the door straight to Network intelligence. Several agents are used only where specialization pays, keeping each prompt small; where it did not, we cut: one Inbox organizer call per text replaced two specialists reading the same text.

One real run, step by step: Plan my week (run c469f1ee77fc, live site, Oct 26)

  1. 0.1 s The student presses Plan my week and the coach starts with 23 open actions that code derived from the records (deadlines, follow-ups, calls): the only actions it may rank. Zero tokens so far.
  2. 2.2 s The coach asks the Opportunity scout about fit, status, deadlines and missing requirements at Meridian Stadiums, Northbridge Sports Group and Keystone Bank. The scout reads the 10 best-matching of 12 opportunity records.
  3. 2.3 s In parallel, it asks Network intelligence about Tom Becker, Luis Moreno, Sofia Alvarez, Hannah Brooks and anyone who could help at Northbridge or Keystone; it reads 10 of 12 people records. At 2.4 s the coach runs the data checks in code: 0 problems.
  4. 12.9 s The scout answers (1,580 input / 0 cached / 626 output tokens, 10.7 s): Meridian is a strong fit at the interview stage, with the second round on Oct 28. 13.8 s Network intelligence answers (1,728 / 0 / 879, 11.5 s): Sofia Alvarez is a strong contact who asked for Jordan's résumé; two others are weak and unanswered.
  5. 22.2 s The coach returns 5 ranked actions, each with why now, first step and record chips, plus 3 watch-outs and 2 questions only the student can answer (2 model calls: 5,801 / 3,634 / 1,258). Code then checks that every action is one of the 23 candidates and every cited id came back from a specialist in this run.

Total: 4 model calls, 9,109 input tokens (3,634 cached), 2,763 output, 22.2 s, $0.0020, the same on screen (Figure 4), on the Usage page (Figure 7) and in screenshots/2026-10-26_live/runs.json.

Two things bound the risk. A hard cap: the daily budget of 300,000 tokens per student limits anyone to $0.15 a day even if every token were output. And hosting, not tokens, dominates at small scale. 6Safety

The app reads text written by strangers (recruiters, web pages, job boards) and holds a student's private relationships. The risks that matter are those that would hurt a student: a hidden instruction changing their plan, a made-up fact, a message sent in their name, their data reaching someone else, a runaway bill. Each defence below is enforced in code, not just requested in a prompt.

RiskDefence actually implemented (where) · shown working
Prompt injection in a paste, email or chatLines that read like instructions to an AI are removed before any model sees them and shown to the student (safety.quarantine, 0 tokens); text reaches the model inside “untrusted” delimiters; if a line slipped through, no agent could send and the keeper would still write only quoted facts. Shown: Figure 8a (live); test injected instructions never reach the model and cannot act.
Hidden text in web pages and job boardsHTML is reduced to what a person would see: white, 1-px, hidden and zero-size text is removed and listed (safety.visible_text); the same for linked pages and careers pages the scout reads. Shown: Figure 8b (live); Figure 5 (20 snippets removed); 2 tests.

Tests, a live check and two adversarial reviews

  • 72 automated tests pass in 1.7 s with no API key and no tokens (fake models stand in for gpt-6-luna; runs/2026-10-25_safety_tests.txt; repeated on a fresh clone on Oct 26). Key ones: injected instructions never reach the model and cannot act; quotes and people not in the text are rejected; no agent has a tool that could send anything; one student cannot see or change another student's records; a looping agent is stopped by the usage limit; the kill switch stops every AI route at zero tokens; an answer from another model is rejected.
  • Live full check, 19 of 19 passed on the deployed site (Oct 26, runs/2026-10-26_live_full_check.json): every agent answered with real tokens, the shield quarantined the injected line, the link reader removed hidden text, the guardrails answered 422, 404 and 401 as designed, and the daily budget was enforced and shown.
  • Two adversarial reviews, with reviewers and skeptics who tried to refute every finding. Auto-sync: 18 findings confirmed (13 distinct), none refuted, all fixed with regression tests (one email in an odd character set crashed the whole run, stopping iMessage and WhatsApp too). Agent team: among others, a paused intake was still acknowledged to the connector, so emails could be lost; tokens of a response crossing a limit went uncounted; a page could create people. All confirmed findings were fixed before the live check.

Honest limits of these defences. The shield is pattern matching, so an instruction worded in a new way could reach the model. That is why the defences are layered: a model that obeyed would still have no tool to send, and nothing it invented would pass the keeper's quote check. A student who pastes a convincing but false posting will get a card for it; the quote shows exactly where it came from.

8Limits and next steps

What does not work yet, and current limitations

  • No real user test yet. The impact numbers are placeholders, and the first tester will be the author, who is also the builder. One tester is not a sample; classmates should follow.
  • Auto-sync of chats is half verified. Under macOS's scheduler Gmail syncs on its own, but iMessage and WhatsApp are refused until the scheduled Python gets Full Disk Access. The refusal and the app's instructions were verified; a real grant was not, so chats may need syncing by hand. It also needs a Mac.
  • Free hosting. Render sleeps after 15 idle minutes (cold start 32 s). A GitHub Action should ping it every 10 minutes from 8 AM to 1 AM Eastern, but GitHub ran it only 4 times on Oct 25–26 (runs/2026-10-26_keep_awake_runs.txt); a free Supabase project pauses after a week without activity.
  • Job boards are found by name. The scout guesses each organisation's board address on Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby and SmartRecruiters from its name, so a board under a different name is missed. Handshake and LinkedIn Jobs forbid scraping, so their postings are pasted.
  • Slack, LinkedIn and Android texts are manual (export or paste), because their owners allow no outside app to read messages.
  • Cost and quality gaps. Portkey's fee is unknown; the monthly usage is an estimate; retrieval recall at scale, the chat filter's misses and the Duolingo miss (Section 5) are unmeasured. The shield is pattern matching (Section 6).
  • Known interface flaws, seen in this session's screenshots: the boss is called “Career strategist” on some screens and “Career coach” on others; the Usage table shows the raw labels “guide” and “discover”; in Figure 4a, item 1's date chip reads Oct 29 while its text says the interview is Oct 28; Robinhood has two cards, the target-list card and the scout's posting; on a phone the tab row runs off the screen.

What we would build next with another month

  1. Measure. Run the protocol with 5 to 10 classmates and publish times, ratings and quotes.
  2. An evaluation set. 50 fictional messages and postings with known answers, scoring extraction, retrieval recall, the chat filter and plan quality on every change (and settling the Duolingo miss).
  3. One-click connection. A signed Mac menu-bar app instead of a Terminal script, with Full Disk Access verified end to end; Gmail through Google's sign-in once the app passes Google's review.
  4. Fix the flaws above: one name for the coach, merged target and posting cards, readable Usage labels, a mobile tab row, and boards matched by the posting links the student already has, not only by name.
  5. A career-office pilot. Always-on hosting ($32 a month), school sign-in, and postings from the school's own platform with its permission.
